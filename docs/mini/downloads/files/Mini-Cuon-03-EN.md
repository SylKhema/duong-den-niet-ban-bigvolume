## Front and back cover
Bìa / Cover

Nibbānagāminipaṭipadā — The Practice Leading to Nibbāna (Abridged). Volume 3. Meditation on Materiality (Rūpakammaṭṭhāna). Pa-Auk Tawya Sayadaw.

Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na rūpaññū hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu “yaṃ kiñci rūpaṃ sabbaṃ rūpaṃ cattāri mahābhūtāni catunnañca mahābhūtānaṃ upādāyarūpan”ti yathābhūtaṃ nappajānāti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na rūpaññū hoti.

Bhikkhus, how does a bhikkhu fail to know materiality? Here, a bhikkhu does not distinguish and know as it really is that all materiality consists of the four great elements and the twenty-four kinds of derived materiality dependent on them. This is how a bhikkhu fails to know materiality. (Ma 1,282.)

So gaṇanato (samuṭṭhānato) rūpaṃ ajānanto rūpaṃ pariggahetvā arūpaṃ vavatthapetvā rūpārūpaṃ pariggahetvā paccayaṃ sallakkhetvā lakkhaṇaṃ āropetvā kammaṭṭhānaṃ matthakaṃ pāpetuṃ na sakkoti.

If a meditating bhikkhu does not know materiality by its number, the twenty-eight kinds, and by its origins, kamma, consciousness, temperature and nutriment, he cannot discern materiality with wisdom, define mentality, discern mentality and materiality together, distinguish their conditions clearly, apply the three characteristics, and bring insight practice to its culmination in path and fruition knowledge. (Ma-ṭṭha 2,63.)

## Meditation on Materiality — Four-elements meditation
Miến / Burmese 1

Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa.
Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Enlightened One.

To distinguish and know materiality, mentality, their causes and effects, that is, the conditioned phenomena in the internal and external continuities across the three periods of time, by the three kinds of full understanding (pariññā), one must first develop one of two kinds of concentration, access concentration or absorption concentration. In the preceding volumes of the Nibbānagāminipaṭipadā (Abridged), the section on tranquillity meditation covered concentration and purification of mind. We now continue with the section on meditation on materiality, explaining how to discern and distinguish with wisdom the material phenomena that come under purification of view.

The brief method of four-elements meditation

Duvidhañhi kammaṭṭhānaṃ — rūpakammaṭṭhānaṃ arūpakammaṭṭhānañca. Rūpapariggaho arūpapariggahotipi etadeva vuccati. Tattha bhagavā rūpakammaṭṭhānaṃ kathento saṅkhepamanasikāravasena vā vitthāramanasikāravasena vā catudhātuvavatthānaṃ katheti. (Abhi-ṭṭha 2,252; Ma-ṭṭha 1,280; Dī-ṭṭha 2,314.)

## Two ways of attending to the four elements
Miến / Burmese 2

The tranquillity section has forty meditation subjects and deals with concentration. The insight section has two kinds of meditation, meditation on materiality and meditation on mentality. These are also called discernment of materiality (rūpapariggaha), distinguishing materiality with wisdom, and discernment of mentality (arūpapariggaha), distinguishing mentality with wisdom. In teaching meditation on materiality, the Buddha teaches the defining of the four elements (catudhātuvavatthāna), distinguishing the individual characteristic of each element with wisdom, either by (1) attention in brief or (2) attention in detail. (Abhi-ṭṭha 2,252; Ma-ṭṭha 1,280; Dī-ṭṭha 2,314.)

This book presents the brief practice of four-elements meditation first and then explains the detailed practice.

Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu imameva kāyaṃ yathāṭhitaṃ yathāpaṇihitaṃ dhātuso paccavekkhati: “Atthi imasmiṃ kāye pathavīdhātu āpodhātu tejodhātu vāyodhātū”ti. (Ma 1,73.)

Bhikkhus, further, a meditating bhikkhu discerns this very body, however it is placed or positioned, by way of the elements: “In this body there is the earth element, the water element, the fire element and the wind element.” He discerns each element in this way. (Ma 1,73.)

The passage above teaches four-elements meditation in the section on attention to the elements in the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta. The meditator should insert the hand of wisdom between the bones and sinews …

## The four great elements
Miến / Burmese 3

… between the flesh and skin of this body. Direct wisdom there to see and know the individual characteristics of earth, water, fire and wind. If these are difficult to see immediately, insert the hand of wisdom between the bones, sinews, flesh and skin, and examine from the head down to the feet and from the feet up to the head as follows.

1. Earth element (pathavīdhātu)
Three coarse qualities: hardness, roughness and heaviness.

## A point about temperature-produced materiality
Miến / Burmese 134

The subcommentary explains that temperature can produce materiality even in the absence of clung-to materiality.
