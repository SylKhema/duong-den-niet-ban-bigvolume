Attaining clear comprehension without delusion
Miến / Burmese 1

Conditional Relations According to the Paṭṭhāna.

Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa.

Evamettha khandhāyatanadhātupaccayapaccavekkhaṇavasenapi asammohasampajaññaṃ veditabbaṃ. (Majjhima Commentary I, p. 268.)

Here, clear comprehension without delusion should also be understood by reviewing the aggregates, bases, elements and conditions.

This section on clear comprehension without delusion presents four methods: (1) by the five aggregates, (2) by the twelve bases, (3) by the eighteen elements, and (4) by examining conditional relations according to the Paṭṭhāna.

The great commentators instructed practitioners to exert themselves by these methods to attain knowledge of clear comprehension without delusion. In accordance with their guidance, this section on conditional relations according to the Paṭṭhāna explains how to examine and discern causal phenomena by the Paṭṭhāna method. It is intended for good people who wish to broaden their understanding through the way the Paṭṭhāna presents the teaching.



Preparation and the great conascence group
Miến / Burmese 2

Anyone wishing to study this section should memorize the Explanation of the Conditions (Paccayaniddesa) in the Paṭṭhāna Pāḷi, its Burmese rendering and the essential meaning of the text. This makes it possible to understand more clearly the nature of the causal relationships set out in the Paṭṭhāna. After studying in this way, memorize the twenty-four Paṭṭhāna conditions presented below.

The twenty-four conditions are presented according to the method handed down by successive teachers, as recorded in the abridged explanation of the Paṭṭhāna by the venerable elder Masoeyein Sayadaw, holder of the title Abhidhajamahāraṭṭhaguru.

Namo Buddhāya.

The twenty-four Paṭṭhāna conditions. The great conascence group comprises four conditions.

The conditioning phenomena for conascence, dependence, presence and non-disappearance conditions are:

1. The eighty-nine consciousnesses and fifty-two mental factors, comprising the four mental aggregates in the course of life and at rebirth-linking, support one another.
2. The four great elements support one another.
3. The four mental aggregates and the heart-base at rebirth-linking in the five-aggregate realm support one another.



Conditioned phenomena of the great and intermediate conascence groups

4. The mental aggregates support consciousness-born materiality and kamma-born materiality at rebirth-linking. They comprise seventy-five consciousnesses, excluding the four immaterial resultant consciousnesses, the ten types of double fivefold sense consciousness and the death consciousness of arahants in the five-aggregate realm, together with the fifty-two mental factors, that is, the four mental aggregates in the course of life and at rebirth-linking.
5. The four great elements support derived materiality.

The conditionally arisen phenomena are: (1) the eighty-nine consciousnesses and fifty-two mental factors, comprising the four mental aggregates in the course of life and at rebirth-linking, supported by one another, (2) the four great elements supported by one another, (3) the four mental aggregates and the heart-base at rebirth-linking in the five-aggregate realm, supported by one another, (4) consciousness-born materiality and kamma-born materiality at rebirth-linking, supported by the mental aggregates, and (5) derived materiality supported by the great elements.

Natural decisive support and summary
Miến / Burmese 10

… the conditioned phenomena are the nine or eight kamma-born material phenomena in the same group, excluding the material life faculty.

For natural decisive support (pakatūpanissaya), the conditioning phenomena are 89 strong consciousnesses, 52 mental factors, 28 material phenomena and certain concepts that can serve as conditions. The conditioned phenomena are 89 consciousnesses and 52 mental factors. This concludes the twenty-four Paṭṭhāna conditions.

Summary of the essential meaning. In the great, intermediate and minor conascence groups, the cause and result arise simultaneously. In the groups of co-arising phenomena each supports the other, like a stake relying on its tether and the tether relying on the stake.

In the object group, the conditioning phenomena are ultimate phenomena of the three periods, namely consciousness, mental factors and materiality, as well as timeless Nibbāna and concepts. They support by serving as objects to be known. The conditioned phenomena are the mental phenomena capable of knowing those objects, namely consciousness and mental factors. One side is the object, the other what takes the object: the object known and the mental phenomena that know it.



Summary of proximity, base, postnascence and asynchronous kamma
Miến / Burmese 11

In the proximity group, the conditioning phenomena are the mental phenomena in the preceding consciousness moment, and the conditioned phenomena are the mental phenomena in the succeeding moment. No phenomenon intervenes between the conditioning and conditioned consciousness moments.

In the physical base group, the conditioning phenomena are the six material bases that have reached presence. The conditioned phenomena are the corresponding mental phenomena. Both belong to the present.

In the postnascence group, the conditioning phenomena are mental phenomena in later consciousness moments. The conditioned phenomena are material phenomena that arose with earlier consciousnesses and have reached presence.

In the asynchronous kamma group, cause and result arise at different times. They can be separated by one consciousness moment, as with preceding and succeeding consciousness in path and fruition, or by many moments, a lifetime, many lifetimes or many aeons.

In the material nutriment group, both the cause and the result are material. This explains how nutritive essence supports nutriment-born materiality…

A point to note carefully

In a noble-path process, the corresponding noble fruition impulsion occurs twice or three times after the noble path. Fruition impulsion immediately after the path is the corresponding noble fruition impulsion that occurs directly after the noble path. To be precise, when noble fruition impulsion occurs twice or three times in a path process, the first is fruition immediately after the path. The second noble fruition impulsion, or the second and third, are fruition impulsions distinct from the one immediately following the path.

Noble persons can enter again the noble fruition they have attained. This is called fruition attainment (phalasamāpatti). Note that the fruition impulsions in that attainment process also belong to the fruition impulsions distinct from the one immediately following the path.
