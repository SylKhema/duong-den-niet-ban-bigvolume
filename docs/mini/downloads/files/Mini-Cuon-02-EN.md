DEVELOP CONCENTRATION

Samādhiṃ, bhikkhave, bhāvetha. Samāhito, bhikkhave, bhikkhu yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. Kiñca yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti? “Idaṃ dukkhan”ti yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. “Ayaṃ dukkhasamudayo”ti yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. “Ayaṃ dukkhanirodho”ti yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. “Ayaṃ dukkhanirodhagāminī paṭipadā”ti yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. Samādhiṃ, bhikkhave, bhāvetha. Samāhito, bhikkhave, bhikkhu yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. (Saṃ, 3, 363.)

Bhikkhus, develop concentration. A bhikkhu who is concentrated understands things as they really are, without distortion. What does he understand as it really is?

He understands as it really is, “This is the Noble Truth of Suffering.” He understands as it really is, “This is the Noble Truth of the Origin of Suffering.” He understands as it really is, “This is the Noble Truth of the Cessation of Suffering—Nibbāna, where suffering ceases.” He understands as it really is, “This is the Noble Truth of the Path Leading to the Cessation of Suffering—Nibbāna.”

Bhikkhus, establish concentration. A bhikkhu who is concentrated understands things as they really are, without distortion. (Saṃyutta Nikāya 3, p. 363.)

## Attention to repulsiveness and the skeleton meditation subject
Miến / Burmese 1

SUBJECTS OF TRANQUILLITY MEDITATION — B

Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa.

Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Enlightened One.

ATTENTION TO REPULSIVENESS AND THE SKELETON MEDITATION SUBJECT
In this second part of tranquillity meditation, I shall continue by briefly explaining how to develop the four protective meditation subjects before proceeding to insight. For most meditators, developing those protective subjects is easier after developing the white kasiṇa, or the ten kasiṇas and eight attainments. This also greatly supports the detailed method of four-elements meditation. Since there are also many methods of discerning the meditation subject of the four elements, I shall first describe how to discern the thirty-two parts of the body, the skeleton meditation subject, and how to proceed from the skeleton to the white kasiṇa and then, successively, through the eight attainments on each kasiṇa.

Re-establish ānāpāna fourth-jhāna concentration until the light of wisdom shines brilliantly. When its light is very strong, use it to discern the thirty-two parts of the body. Divide the twenty body parts with predominant earth element into four groups of five, and the twelve with predominant water element into two groups of six. Begin with one group at a time. When, looking in a clear mirror, you can see your own face distinctly, you should be able to see the thirty-two parts just as distinctly. If the light of knowledge fades and the body parts become unclear while discerning them, re-establish ānāpāna fourth-jhāna concentration…

## The three entrances
Miến / Burmese 4

…At the nearest place reached by that light, especially directly in front, discern the thirty-two body parts in the continuity of a good person or another living being. Repeatedly discern forwards from head hair down to urine and backwards from urine up to head hair. When successful, alternate once internally and once externally, again and again. Only when you can repeatedly alternate internally and externally does the strength of development increase.

Following this method, gradually discern from near to far, extending throughout all ten directions. Examine each person or animal reached by the light of ānāpāna fourth jhāna, one being after another, alternating internally and externally. If, whenever you discern, you no longer see a man, woman, buffalo, cow, or animal, but only a collection of thirty-two body parts, you have become thoroughly familiar and proficient, internally and externally (paguṇa).

THREE ENTRANCES

Koṭṭhāsānaṃ paguṇakālato paṭṭhāya tīsu mukhesu ekena mukhena vimuccissati, kammaṭṭhānaṃ vaṇṇato vā paṭikūlato vā suññato vā upaṭṭhāti. Pe. Ākaṅkhamānassa vaṇṇato ākaṅkhamānassa paṭikūlato ākaṅkhamānassa suññato kammaṭṭhānaṃ upaṭṭhahissatiyeva. (Abhi-ṭṭha, 2, 242–243.)

<a id="M02-body-P131"></a>
## Mindfulness of death
Miến / Burmese 131

Tattha maraṇanti ekabhavapariyāpannassa jīvitindriyassa upacchedo.

Pe. Iti jīvitindriyupacchedasaṅkhātassa maraṇassa saraṇaṃ maraṇassati. (Visuddhimagga I, 222.)

Here death means the cutting off of the life faculty within a single existence. Mindfulness of death is the recollection of death as the cutting off of the life faculty.

Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu seyyathāpi passeyya sarīraṃ sivathikāya chaḍḍitaṃ ekāhamataṃ vā dvīhamataṃ vā tīhamataṃ vā uddhumātakaṃ vinīlakaṃ vipubbakajātaṃ. So imameva kāyaṃ upasaṃharati: “Ayampi kho kāyo evaṃdhammo evaṃbhāvī evaṃanatīto”ti. (Ma 1,73.)

Again, bhikkhus, as though seeing a corpse thrown into a charnel ground, one day, two days, or three days dead, swollen, discoloured, and festering, a bhikkhu compares this very body with it: “This body too is of that nature, will become like that, and has not gone beyond that.”

Tasmā tattha hatamatādisatte oloketvā diṭṭhapubbasampattīnaṃ sattānaṃ matānaṃ maraṇaṃ āvajjetvā satiñca saṃvegañca ñāṇañca yojetvā “maraṇaṃ bhavissatī”tiādinā nayena manasikāro pavattetabbo. (Visuddhi 1,223.)

Therefore, look at beings who have been killed or have died here and there, and bring to mind the death of beings who died amid prosperity. With mindfulness, a sense of urgency, and discernment, attend in such ways as “Death will come.”

Following these passages, to develop mindfulness of death, first re-establish concentration on foulness up to the first jhāna. When you can contemplate the repulsive corpse up to first-jhāna concentration, direct discernment to your own death: “This body of mine too is subject to such death. This death will surely occur. I cannot go beyond it.” Looking with mindfulness, urgency, and discernment, you will see your own repulsive corpse. Then use discernment to see the cutting off of the life faculty in that image of your own death…


<a id="M02-body-P132"></a>

…Keep the developing mind peacefully settled on the cutting off of the life faculty, attending as follows.

1. Maraṇaṃ me dhuvaṃ, jīvitaṃ me adhuvaṃ. My death is certain, my life uncertain.
2. Maraṇaṃ me bhavissati. Death will come to me.
3. Maraṇapariyosānaṃ me jīvitaṃ. My life ends in death.
4. Maraṇaṃ, maraṇaṃ. Death, death.

Choose whichever of these ways of attending you prefer, in Pāli or your own language. Strive to keep the mind developing mindfulness of death peacefully settled for an hour or two on the cutting off of the life faculty in the image of your death. When it remains settled in this way, the five jhāna factors will become evident. This is access jhāna only.

The cutting off of the life faculty

This is a difficult contemplation because it concerns an ultimate reality, the life faculty. The Visuddhimagga defines mindfulness of death as recollecting death as the cutting off of the life faculty. Practise so as to see the cutting off of your own life faculty. Merely bringing to mind and seeing your own corpse with discernment does not complete the task. Therefore, once the meditator…


<a id="M02-body-P133"></a>
## Discerning the cessation of the life faculty
Miến / Burmese 133

…has developed first-jhāna concentration with a repulsive corpse as object, they can direct discernment towards the image of their own death. When you see your corpse, direct attention inside its heart. You may not immediately see the cutting off of the life faculty, since in that corpse the faculty has already been cut off.

When you can direct discernment into the heart in this way, look back a little to the time before death. You may then see, near the time of death, the clarity of the life-continuum mind-door or the stream of consciousness. Here the intended object is the clarity of the life-continuum mind-door. Since you have already discerned it when discerning the jhāna factors, you can discern it readily here too. When you see it with discernment, move closer to the time of death. You can then see that clarity cease. As explained earlier, discern the life-continuum consciousness that produces that clarity. When you see it, look further to see the cutting off of the life faculty that accompanies it. In seeing that, you understand the cutting off of the life faculty.

For most meditators who have never discerned mentality, however, at this stage it is very difficult to understand the life-continuum consciousness and the life faculty.


<a id="M02-body-P134"></a>
## The four protective meditation subjects
Miến / Burmese 134

Even if this is difficult, you have already seen the clarity of the life-continuum mind-door and can develop mindfulness of death by taking its cessation as object. As explained above, this clarity is called the life-continuum by way of a figurative designation. With discernment you can still bring your own corpse into view. Someone who has practised mindfulness of death in past lives may see the cutting off of the life faculty, or, put another way, the cessation of the life-continuum consciousness together with the life faculty. It is best if this can be seen.

Most meditators who have yet to discern mentality can see the cessation of the clarity of the life-continuum mind-door just explained, called the life-continuum figuratively. They can develop mindfulness of death by taking as object the cessation of that material clarity. Meditators who have discerned the relations of cause and effect in dependent origination up to knowledge of discerning conditions can readily see the cessation of the life-continuum consciousness together with the life faculty.

Loving-kindness, recollection of the Buddha, foulness, and mindfulness of death are the four protective meditation subjects. They are called protective because they guard meditators. They are subjects to learn and cultivate before proceeding to insight.


<a id="M02-body-P135"></a>
## Choosing a subject to counter a hindrance
Miến / Burmese 135

Asubhā bhāvetabbā rāgassa pahānāya.
Mettā bhāvetabbā byāpādassa pahānāya.
Ānāpānassati bhāvetabbā vitakkupacchedāya. (Aṅ 3,169; Udāna 120, Meghiyasutta.)

Develop foulness for abandoning lust. Develop loving-kindness for abandoning ill will. Develop mindfulness of breathing for cutting off applied thoughts.

According to this teaching in the Meghiya Sutta, foulness is an excellent means of countering lust. The foulness meditation described above, with a corpse as its object, is foulness connected with the lifeless. The Girimānanda Sutta teaches another subject of foulness, attending to the thirty-two parts of a living body as repulsive. This is foulness connected with the living. Both kinds are excellent means of removing lust. Loving-kindness is an excellent subject for removing ill will and aversion. Mindfulness of breathing is an effective subject for countering plans and other kinds of thought.

Therefore, good meditator, develop foulness when lust is strong, loving-kindness when aversion is strong, and mindfulness of breathing when thoughts proliferate. When faith diminishes and the mind lacks clarity in meditation, develop recollection of the Buddha. When urgency diminishes and you become lax in meditation, develop mindfulness of death.

## Conclusion of the section on tranquillity meditation
Miến / Burmese 136

The Buddha taught forty meditation subjects for tranquillity. Of these, this section has presented the following subjects in brief.

1. Mindfulness of breathing.
2. Attention to repulsiveness, the meditation on the bones.
3. The ten kasiṇas and the eight attainments.
4. The four divine abidings.
5. Recollection of the Buddha.
6. Meditation on foulness.
7. Mindfulness of death.

Anyone wishing to cultivate the remaining tranquillity subjects should approach teachers and learn from them. If you wish to proceed to insight meditation, continue by developing the subjects beginning with the discernment of materiality.
